Anata Discovery & sales call script

Seventeen sections in call order, each with a question bank and color-coded talk tracks. Jump with the left sidebar. Run the Close tracker on the right as the prospect talks.

How to use this script

This script follows Anata's call order, and every section is tagged with the belief(s) it builds and how it widens the gap. Each section has 10 questions; you will not ask all 10. Pick the 1-2 that fit the conversation, then dig with follow-ups. The banks are there so you never get stuck and never sound scripted. The close is Anata Intelligence at $99/month with a 14-day free trial; full service is the step-up when the call calls for it.

The goal of discovery: the prospect says all 7 beliefs out loud, in their own words, before you pitch. When they hear themselves say it, consistency bias does the closing for you.

The 7 beliefs

Belief What the prospect must believe Objection it kills
Pain "I have a real problem keeping me from what I want." "We're fine, just exploring."
Doubt "I can't fix this myself, or doing it myself wastes time and money." DIY / "my team can handle it"
Cost "Doing nothing costs me far more than fixing it." "Not right now" / timing
Desire "Solving this gets me a much better future." Low motivation / indifference
Money "I have the resources and I'm willing to use them on this." Price (willingness side)
Support "My partner / team will back this decision." "I need to talk to my partner"
Trust "This method is different and better than what burned me before." "I've been burned by agencies and tools"

Widen the gap: the principles

The gap is the distance between where the brand is today and where the founder wants it to be. The wider the prospect sees that gap, the more urgent the decision feels. You widen it from both ends.

  • Push the current situation down: make the pain specific (numbers), long (how long it's been this way), and spread (impact on other parts of the business and their personal life).
  • Pull the desired situation up: get a specific number, the reason behind it, and what hitting it would change personally.
  • Never tell them the gap is big. Ask questions that make them say it. A gap they describe is a gap they believe.
  • Use a pained tone when they share bad numbers. Tone says "this is serious" without you saying it.
  • Connect the gap to Anata only at the end. In discovery you are a doctor diagnosing, not a salesperson prescribing.

Rules for running the call

  1. 1-2 question rule: one big question from the bank, then 1-2 small digging questions ("Tell me more", "What do you mean by that?", "Why is that?").
  2. Don't create doubt that's already there. If they've told you they can't figure it out, note the belief and move on.
  3. Leave Money to the end. If you've built the other 6 beliefs and they have the resources, they usually close.
  4. Take notes in their exact words. You'll repeat them back in the pitch: "You know how earlier you told me..."
  5. Tick the belief meter in the Close tracker as you go. Every box checked before the pitch is one fewer objection at the close.

Live-call tips

This page is a reference, not a teleprompter. These habits make it work under pressure.

  1. Prep 5 minutes before every call. Pick the 2 closest case studies to the prospect's category (skincare or beauty: Sonrei or Trestique; supplements and health: SeroVital, Novex Biotech or Zantrex; apparel: Arctic Cool; starting from scratch: Ocean Rx; "we're already fine": Sonrei). Write their names on your notepad.
  2. Run the Close tracker on the right. Tick beliefs as the prospect says them, fill in the gap, and tap each pain point, objection and reservation they raise. The talk flow rebuilds instantly. Everything stays in this browser until you press "New call".
  3. Jump, don't scroll. Click a section in the sidebar, press / to search, or use J and K to move to the next or previous section.
  4. Read the red line, then go silent. After a widen-the-gap line, wait at least 3 seconds. The prospect fills the silence with the real pain.
  5. One case study per section, maximum. Stacking proof sounds like bragging. One relevant story beats five generic ones.
  6. Swap in their numbers. Use the Pain Island calculator with what they told you in Section 5, out loud, so it's their math, not yours.
  7. Personalize your bank. Highlight your 3 favorite questions per section on your notepad. After 10 calls, delete the ones you never use.
  8. Review after every call. Note which belief was missing when a deal stalled. Patterns show up within 2 weeks.
  9. Record and replay one call a week. Mark where you pitched before all 7 beliefs were checked. That's the leak.
  10. Keep claims accurate. Use case-study numbers exactly as written. If a prospect asks for proof, offer anatainc.com/case-studies rather than improvising. Never quote a result that is not in this script.

1Isolate the problem

Builds: Pain · Gap move: mark the X, the spot where we start digging.

Opening line: "That being said, probably our best place to start is: what's the biggest challenge in your Amazon business right now? What's not working at the level it truly could be, or should be?"

If there's no problem, there's no sale. Get one clear problem named in their words before anything else, then keep the whole call anchored to it.

Question bank (pick 1, then dig with 1-2 follow-ups):

  1. What's the biggest challenge in your Amazon business right now?
  2. If you could wave a magic wand and fix one thing in the business tomorrow, what would it be?
  3. What made you book this call now, rather than three months ago or three months from now?
  4. What's the one number in your account that frustrates you most when you look at it?
  5. Where do you feel the business is underperforming compared to what it should be doing?
  6. What's keeping you up at night when it comes to the brand?
  7. What's the gap between where the brand is today and where you expected it to be by now?
  8. When you look at your ads, listings and margins, which one feels most broken?
  9. Is the biggest issue getting sales, or making the sales you get profitable?
  10. If nothing changed in the next 12 months, what's the problem you'd be most frustrated hadn't been solved?

Listen for: a specific problem (high TACoS, flat sales, thin margins, lost rank, no idea which SKUs actually make money). Write it down word for word. Mark the X: "Okay, so the big one is . Let's dig into that."

2Background questions

Builds: rapport, plus early clues for Desire, Support and Money · Gap move: sets the baseline you'll measure the gap against.

Ask no more than 3-4 of these. Background warms up the conversation before you cut back to the pain. Listen for "we" and "us" (Support), why they started (Desire), and which channels they sell on (Intelligence plan size, full-service fit).

Question bank:

  1. Tell me about the product line. What do you sell?
  2. How did the brand get started? Why did you form it?
  3. How long has the brand been live on Amazon?
  4. How many ASINs do you have right now, and which ones drive most of the revenue?
  5. Is Amazon your main channel, or are you also selling on Shopify, TikTok Shop, wholesale or retail?
  6. Who's on the team today, and who handles ads, listings and reporting day to day?
  7. How are you fulfilling today: FBA, your own warehouse, or a 3PL?
  8. What's the brand's best month ever been, and what was happening then?
  9. What are you most proud of in the brand so far?
  10. Where does Amazon fit in your long-term plan: build and hold, or build and exit?

Transition back to pain: "Awesome, appreciate the background. So going back to what you said about , tell me more."

3Current situation

Builds: Pain · Gap move: pushes the current situation down by making the problem concrete.

Reference the problem they isolated in Section 1. Turn a vague complaint ("ads aren't working") into a specific, felt problem ("we're losing money on every ad sale and I don't know why").

Question bank:

  1. Tell me more. What do you mean when you say ?
  2. Why do you say that's the biggest challenge, over everything else?
  3. Walk me through what that looks like on a normal week in the business.
  4. When did you first notice this was a problem?
  5. How do you know it's a problem? What are you seeing in the numbers?

  9. What do you think is actually causing it?

4Assess current efforts

Builds: Pain, early Doubt · Gap move: shows effort is already going in and the gap still isn't closing.

Opening line: "Gotcha. So what are you currently doing now to grow your Amazon brand and get sales?"

Question bank:

5The numbers

Builds: Pain (quantified), quiet clues for Money · Gap move: puts a hard number on the current situation so the gap can be measured.

Opening line: "Okay, great. So let's see how that's working out for you. Last month, how much did you spend on ads?"

Numbers make pain undeniable. If they don't know a number, that's a finding in itself: "Totally fine. Does it worry you a little that you don't know that one?" That's exactly what Anata Intelligence answers. Fill in the gap table as you go.

Question bank:

  1. Last month, how much did you spend on ads?
  2. What were total sales last month, in units and revenue?
  3. How much of that revenue came from ads, and what was your ACoS?
  4. So what does that put your TACoS at? How do you feel about that number?

6How long

Builds: Cost · Gap move: stretches the pain across time. A bad month is a blip; a bad year is a pattern.

Opening line (pained tone, "wow, that's rough"): "Gotcha. So how long have your sales / TACoS / growth rate been like this?"

The longer the problem has existed, the more it has already cost them, and the less likely it is to fix itself.

Question bank:

  1. How long have your numbers been like this?
  2. When was the last time things felt like they were really working?
  3. What changed between then and now?
  4. Over that stretch, roughly how much do you think this has cost you in lost profit?
  5. How many months have you been telling yourself "next month it'll turn around"?
  6. Has it gotten better, worse or stayed flat over that time?
  7. How long have you known this needed fixing before reaching out?

  9. How much longer can the business realistically run like this?
  10. What did you expect the numbers to look like by now when you first launched?
